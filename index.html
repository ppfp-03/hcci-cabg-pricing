Trade-offs

  • Price stickiness. Rates hard to raise after insurer contracts (p. 10).
  • Unsigned demand. Volume depends on unsigned contracts and on JCI accreditation in 2015 (p. 9).
  • Partner conflict. Ascension rates with Caribbean insurers (p. 8).
  • Mission and capacity. The reserve turns away a few paying patients in year 5.
  • Package risk. Complication costs stay with HCCI.

Method

CABG profit
(price − $2,000 variable cost) × paying patients − 30% of fixed costs
Fixed costs
$10.0M staff + $4.0M overhead + $6.56M capital a year ($70M at 8% over 25 years)
Paying patients
Demand at the price, up to 600 places minus reserved places
Calibration
About 200 cases in year 1 and 450 in year 5 at around $50,000 (p. 10)
Segment demand
150 Caymanian, 200 US, 100 Caribbean insured patients, falling to zero at $102k, $76k and $80k
Low-sensitivity demand
Elasticity 0.8 at $50,000
Units
2014 dollars, no inflation; recomputed in the browser with the formulas of the Python model
